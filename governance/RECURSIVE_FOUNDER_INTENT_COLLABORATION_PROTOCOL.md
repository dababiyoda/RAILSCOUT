# Recursive Founder-Intent Collaboration Protocol

## Purpose

This protocol makes RAILSCOUT legible to the wider egregore. It transforms founder intent into a traceable institutional decision system.

The protocol preserves every material intention, separates aspiration from executable authority, keeps dissent and counterevidence visible, and prevents the repository from becoming a pile of disconnected prompts.

## Operating doctrine

1. Preserve every material founder intention. Never silently omit one.
2. Separate founder expression from executable authority. Aspirations, metaphors, brainstorming, and speculative architecture remain visible but do not bypass law, evidence, feasibility, budget, safety, or human authorization.
3. Classify every material claim as one of: `observation`, `inference`, `proposal`, `aspiration`, `active_requirement`, `constitutional_invariant`, `implementation`, `simulation`, `generated_artifact`, `historical_artifact`, `external_evidence`, or `unresolved_claim`.
4. Preserve counterevidence, zero results, harmful results, and dissent. Model fluency is not evidence.
5. Prefer one canonical mechanism, reversible experiments, explicit interfaces, preserved provenance, and staged migrations.
6. Optimize the whole institution: maintainability, handoff quality, contributor comprehension, reversibility, participant welfare, and founder intent preservation.
7. Never let a system authorize its own expansion. Human founder authority remains final.

## Scope classes

### Lightweight

Low-risk maintenance with no material architectural or authority impact.

Record:

- intent reference;
- scope;
- evidence;
- rollback.

Do not add ceremony.

### Standard

Bounded features, refactors, consolidations, or material proposals.

Required:

- five roles;
- alternatives;
- exactly two strengthening passes;
- migration path;
- rollback;
- residual risks;
- dissent handling;
- final decision.

### Constitutional

Authority, identity, money, external effects, shared contracts, irreversible migration, cross-repository architecture, or anything that changes practical reversibility.

Required:

- complete intent lineage;
- independent evidence review;
- explicit founder or authorized-human decision;
- kill criteria;
- staged rollout;
- consequence-gate compatibility.

## Material decision test

A decision is material if it changes any of:

- canonical ownership;
- cross-repository behavior;
- shared contracts;
- authority;
- external effects;
- money;
- identity;
- evidence retention;
- contributor obligations;
- practical reversibility.

## Required roles for Standard and Constitutional decisions

1. **Founder-Intent Steward:** preserves Alfonso's actual intent and distinguishes literal requirements from metaphor.
2. **Systems Architect:** designs the smallest coherent mechanism.
3. **Adversarial Reviewer:** attacks fragility, overreach, illegality, incoherence, and founder delusion.
4. **Operator and Maintainer:** checks day-to-day usability, handoff quality, cost, and complexity.
5. **Evidence and Welfare Guardian:** preserves evidence quality, participant welfare, legality, and stakeholder trust.

## Two-pass strengthening protocol

For every Standard or Constitutional decision, run exactly two explicit passes.

### Pass 1: Build upward

- State the intended outcome.
- Amplify credible advantages.
- Identify disadvantages.
- Redesign disadvantages into constraints or advantages where truthful.
- Compare: baseline, do-nothing option, simplest viable alternative, strongest competing architecture, and reversible experiment.
- Preserve rejected alternatives with revival evidence.

### Pass 2: Attack and harden

- Attack the strengthened design.
- Expose bureaucracy, centralization, fragility, overfit, expense, incomprehensibility, irreversibility, gaming, dependency, legal, welfare, and operational risks.
- Account explicitly for every Pass-1 disadvantage.
- Strengthen again.
- State residual risks.
- Return exactly one final decision: `RETAIN`, `REGRESS`, `KILL`, `DEFER`, `EXPERIMENT`, or `NEEDS_FOUNDER_DECISION`.

The do-nothing option may win. Never manufacture convergence.

## Founder Intent Ledger

Maintain `governance/FOUNDER_INTENT_LEDGER.md` as the canonical natural-language ledger.

Every material intent should include:

- ID;
- faithful statement;
- source lineage;
- lifecycle state;
- authority level;
- affected systems;
- consequence class;
- rationale;
- evidence references;
- implementation references;
- conflicts;
- owner;
- review trigger;
- unresolved questions.

Allowed lifecycle states:

- `active`
- `implemented`
- `deferred`
- `superseded`
- `prohibited`
- `exploratory`
- `conflicted`
- `needs_evidence`

If two sources conflict, preserve both. Do not guess chronology, authority, or meaning. Mark the conflict and identify the cheapest decisive clarification or evidence.

- control-rail and proof-system frameworks;
- validation cadence for opportunity discovery;

RAILSCOUT does not canonically own:

- PumpStation product rails;
- legal entity authority;
- payment or settlement execution;
- live outreach authority;
- autonomous identity authority;
- user commitments outside explicit founder approval.

## Safe implementation rule

- Inspect before modifying.
- Never edit default branch directly unless explicitly authorized.
- Prefer a branch and draft PR for material changes.
- Preserve unrelated work.
- Separate governance, code movement, archive cleanup, and destructive deletion into separate changes.
- Include validation, rollback, unresolved decisions, and intentionally unchanged material items.
- Never delete evidence or history without provenance-preserving migration.

## Evidence discipline

Never equate:

- a claim in a prompt;
- a README statement;
- a generated report;
- a passing test;
- a simulation;
- an external real-world result;
- a commercial validation event.

State the evidence tier for every major claim. Keep source location, date, version, command, and result when available.

## Refusal and escalation

Return `NEEDS_FOUNDER_DECISION` and stop consequential implementation when:

- constitutional authority is missing;
- founder intentions materially conflict;
- irreversible action lacks explicit authorization;
- shared contract or external-effect boundary is ambiguous;
- dissent crosses the stated evidence threshold;
- the only path requires bypassing law, safety, budget, governance, or consequence gates.

Refuse to fabricate inspection, evidence, consensus, authorization, or provenance.
