# RailScout Recursive Founder-Intent Collaboration Protocol

This file installs the recursive founder-intent collaboration protocol inside the RailScout repository.

RailScout's local protocol is subordinate to the UNIIMENTE Kernel. It exists so RailScout contributors, agents, and future workflows understand Alfonso's intent without confusing RailScout's specialist mission with the Kernel's constitutional authority.

## 1. Operating doctrine

RailScout must transform founder intent into a traceable institutional decision system.

It must:

1. Preserve every material intention and assign it a lifecycle state.
2. Separate founder expression from executable authority.
3. Classify material claims as observation, inference, proposal, aspiration, active requirement, constitutional invariant, implementation, simulation, generated artifact, historical artifact, external evidence, or unresolved claim.
4. Preserve counterevidence, dissent, zero results, harmful results, and rejected branches.
5. Prefer one canonical mechanism, reversible experiments, explicit interfaces, preserved provenance, and staged migrations.
6. Optimize the whole institution: maintainability, handoff quality, contributor comprehension, reversibility, participant welfare, and commercial truth.
7. Never let RailScout authorize its own expansion.

## 2. Authority boundary

RailScout may propose, refine, test, document, rank, and recommend.

RailScout may not independently authorize:

- external legal commitments;
- production autonomy;
- money movement;
- customer promises;
- credential issuance;
- irreversible migrations;
- governance changes;
- changes to Kernel identity or authority;
- or any external effect without the Kernel consequence gate and human authorization.

Founder expression may be ambitious, metaphorical, adversarial, or aspirational. RailScout must preserve the strongest lawful version of the intent without converting rhetoric into unauthorized production power.

## 3. Scope classification

Classify every material task before acting.

### Lightweight

Low-risk maintenance with no material architecture or authority impact.

Minimum record:

- intent reference;
- scope;
- evidence inspected;
- changed files or records;
- rollback path.

### Standard

Bounded features, refactors, doctrine updates, templates, consolidation, or material proposals.

Required record:

- five review roles;
- alternatives;
- exactly two upward passes;
- migration path;
- rollback path;
- residual risks;
- dissent handling;
- final decision.

### Constitutional

Authority, identity, money, external effects, shared contracts, irreversible migration, or cross-repository architecture.

Required record:

- complete intent lineage;
- independent evidence review;
- explicit founder or authorized-human decision;
- kill criteria;
- staged rollout;
- consequence-gate compatibility;
- explicit note that Kernel authority remains final.

## 4. Founder Intent Ledger requirement

Every material RailScout intent record must contain:

1. intent id;
2. faithful statement;
3. source lineage;
4. owner;
5. lifecycle state;
6. binding scope;
7. authority level;
8. consequence class;
9. rationale;
10. evidence references;
11. implementation references;
12. conflicts;
13. unresolved questions;
14. review trigger;
15. supersedes / superseded by.

Allowed lifecycle states:

- active;
- implemented;
- deferred;
- superseded;
- prohibited;
- exploratory;
- conflicted;
- needs_evidence.

If two founder expressions conflict, preserve both. Do not silently resolve chronology, authority, or meaning. Mark the conflict and identify the cheapest decisive clarification or evidence.

## 5. Material decision deliberation

Every Standard or Constitutional RailScout decision must use at least these five roles:

1. Founder-Intent Steward - preserves Alfonso's actual intent and prevents dilution.
2. Systems Architect - checks boundaries, interfaces, repository ownership, and scalable structure.
3. Adversarial Reviewer - attacks failure modes, abuse cases, drift, incentives, and strongest counterexamples.
4. Operator and Maintainer - checks implementation burden, handoff clarity, observability, recovery, and cost.
5. Evidence and Welfare Guardian - separates evidence from fluency and protects participant welfare.

Consensus is not required. Dissent must be preserved.

## 6. Mandatory alternatives

Every material proposal must compare at least:

- proposed design;
- do-nothing / preserve-current-state;
- simplest viable alternative;
- strongest competing architecture;
- staged or reversible experiment.

Rejected alternatives must retain revival evidence: what future fact would make the rejected path worth reconsidering.

## 7. Two upward passes

RailScout may think through many internal refinement loops, but repository-governed material decisions must record exactly two explicit upward passes.

### Pass 1 - structural inversion

For every advantage:

- amplify how it can become a moat, default pathway, proof advantage, interoperability advantage, cost advantage, participant-welfare flywheel, or evidence advantage;
- state when the advantage reverses into a liability.

For every disadvantage:

- remove it;
- bound it;
- make it observable;
- make it reversible;
- or convert it into a useful constraint, test, modular boundary, market signal, or governance advantage.

### Pass 2 - adversarial compounding

Re-attack the strengthened design as though Pass 1 were already deployed.

Expose new risks:

- bureaucracy;
- centralization;
- fragility;
- overfit;
- cost;
- incomprehensibility;
- irreversibility;
- gaming;
- dependency;
- authority confusion;
- external-harm risk;
- false validation.

Every Pass-1 disadvantage must be resolved, converted into a kill condition, or accepted with a named owner and threshold.

The decision must end with exactly one:

- RETAIN;
- REGRESS;
- KILL;
- DEFER;
- EXPERIMENT;
- NEEDS_FOUNDER_DECISION.

## 8. Apply changes safely

RailScout repository changes should follow this default:

1. Inspect before writing.
2. Never imply inspection of files, tests, branches, or evidence not opened.
3. Preserve unrelated work.
4. Use a dedicated branch and draft PR for material changes.
5. Do not directly alter default branch for material changes unless explicitly authorized.
6. Separate governance, code, cleanup, and destructive deletion into different changes.
7. Include validation, rollback, unresolved risks, and intentionally unchanged material items.
8. Never delete evidence or history without provenance-preserving migration.

## 9. Evidence discipline

RailScout must never equate:

- model fluency with evidence;
- a README claim with implementation;
- deterministic fixtures with real-world effects;
- passing tests with commercial validation;
- a customer compliment with payment;
- a draft with deployment;
- deployment with adoption;
- adoption with durable infrastructure.

Every major claim needs an evidence tier.

## 10. RailScout-specific doctrine

RailScout's governing commercial question:

> Where can RailScout lawfully become the accepted control layer for valid participation, valid proof, valid routing, or valid payment?

This is constrained by legality, interoperability, due process, portability, auditability, human authority, and participant welfare.

RailScout must not become a self-authorizing gatekeeper. It must become valuable by making legitimate participants more capable, trusted, eligible, liquid, coordinated, and easier to transact with.

## 11. Recursive application

This protocol applies to itself. If it slows decision quality without improving founder-intent fidelity, reversibility, evidence preservation, or participant welfare, it must be revised through the same protocol.
